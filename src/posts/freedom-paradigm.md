title: My freedom paradigm
date: 2026-10-12
summary: My thoughts on financial freedom as a 23 year old programmer.
---

You might not know me, and that's fine. I am just here to talk about my experience of chasing money on a daily basis. From a young age I have always set high expectations at everything I did. With access to social media this small bug kept being implanted in my brain of a free life. However, freedom is relative. Some people consider freedom from a perspective of not being bound by people, others financially.

I have always looked at different ways of making money, from content creation, even my career choices are mostly influenced heavily by financial motives. Yes, I love coding, but is there anything else that I'd enjoy more out there but would pay less? Most likely.
However, I never got the chance to even explore those options since I always looked at things logically... I like it, it's good money, that's the goal. But never the end-goal.

My version of freedom comes mainly from finances. If you have money, you have freedom. You can buy anything which means you can do anything (legal of course)? But where did this ideal come from, is it from being raised in a financially unstable environment, is it from all the larp content being fed to me on the timeline. I will never know. As I sit here in my room, with a new PC that I built and bought with my own hands I thought I would be content. But it seems it is never enough.

Yes I have stability for now... not guaranteed stability — if such a thing even exists. In my head it makes sense, if you have enough money you have guaranteed stability.

### Progress?

Have I made any progress? From an outsider perspective I have worked a lot, studied hard, got a master's, got a good job, paying my own bills. However, for me it seems like this is the bare minimum. I started off at the young age of 14...13? I don't honestly remember. I made minecraft plugins, created my own servers, tried monetising those. Then looked into CS:GO cheats, learnt a bit of C++ and C# for those. However, always started, but never finished. It seems at slightest inconvenience I always end up quitting (is this a pattern I see?). Went into university, tried dropshipping, with some friends that never cared enough, I felt like I was the only one that cared about it but since my friends quit so did I. But why? Then I got a job at a restaurant, I was content... had money for my own things, but the more I woke up to go there, the more I dreaded it, the more I needed a way out. I handled working and studying until the completion of my studies. Then I got my first job as an SDE. Good pay, stable(?). I feel content, but not because I want to, because I need to. So many ideas keep circling my head, money, creating an app, creating an AI, becoming the next revolution in some never seen before tech... all bugs that keep crawling in and out. Read a book on finances (yes, a singular book) and invested for 6 months, then liquidated everything to pay off my student debt.
I want to become a better engineer, but I want to enjoy life, but I also want to make money on a side hustle. All of these need to happen at the same time, but I can't be bothered to start any of them.

### My paradigm

Consistency. That's the key I am missing, I want stability, but I want risky adventures, I want freedom but I can't commit to freedom. I am comfortable, but I am not. This is my paradigm. I want to code something, tell myself I will, no AI, so I can become a cool programmer that knows how to left shift and right shift a binary number and also be able to pay attention to the off by 1 bit problem. But I also want to own a yacht, with some business that runs itself, that I do not touch (ever). Not going to happen... the realistic side of me knows most of my reality will be confined within a 9-5. But my brainwashed side does not seem to accept it. Because of this my priorities are always dwindling. I know many of people my age and younger think the same. Perhaps not from a coding perspective but on the freedom side. I have recently started a channel, uploaded shorts, consistently, for 3 months, saw I was 15% of the way of getting monetised, suddenly I couldn't do it anymore and gave up. Not because I was tired, maybe because I didn't enjoy the content I was putting out. This felt way easier to write then the hellish 10 minutes I'd spend editting another animal funny short compilation (yes I know).

### Going forward

Going forward I believe I need a realistic outcome. Something that is based in reality, rather then other people's fantasies. I want to program more, but is it what I really want? I guess the future will tell, I know I will embark in many different endeavours, businesses, start ideas, see that another company did it and quit. But I also know that if something works, even for a slight second I will keep going on it until I scale it.

Thank you for reading this,
This has been my freedom paradigm.
